faq:
  - q: Does OnBudget need me to create custom fields in Jira?
    a: >-
      No. OnBudget reads what is already there: story points, worklogs, existing numeric fields,
      statuses and resolutions. There is nothing to add to a screen scheme and nothing to backfill,
      which is why a first report takes minutes rather than a change request.
  - q: What can OnBudget use to calculate cost?
    a: >-
      Five signals. Story points priced per point, any numeric field priced per unit, worklogs priced
      by a rate card or a flat rate, work items closed or resolved priced per item, and work items
      sitting in chosen statuses priced per item. You can also leave the unit cost blank and let
      OnBudget divide the total budget across the total quantity.
  - q: How does OnBudget know what an hour of work costs?
    a: >-
      Through rate cards. A card holds rates per user or per project role, plus a fallback hourly rate
      for authors it does not recognise. Project admins can define reusable worklog role presets from
      the project settings, so the same roles do not have to be rebuilt for every report.
  - q: What if my team does not use story points or worklogs?
    a: >-
      Then count work items instead. OnBudget can price items closed or resolved, or items sitting in
      statuses you choose. This is the common case for marketing, support and operations, and the
      builder tells you what share of your data carries each signal before you pick one.
  - q: Which currencies are supported, and does OnBudget convert between them?
    a: >-
      Eighteen ISO currencies, including USD, EUR, GBP and BRL. Each report is denominated in one
      currency and OnBudget does not convert between them, because an invented exchange rate is worse
      than no exchange rate. Number, decimal and date formats are set per report alongside it.
  - q: Can I share a report with someone who has limited Jira access?
    a: >-
      Yes, with users or with groups. A shared report is not a snapshot of your view. It regenerates
      under the viewer's own Jira permissions, so nobody sees work items they could not already see in
      Jira, and the report warns when a viewer lacks access to part of its scope.
  - q: Where does my data live?
    a: >-
      Inside Atlassian. OnBudget runs entirely on Forge, is Runs on Atlassian, and holds three
      read-only scopes. It stores your report settings and never the content of your work items or the
      results of a report. Account and group identifiers are hashed before use as storage keys, and
      uninstalling purges everything.
  - q: Can I export the numbers?
    a: >-
      Yes, as CSV with the per work item breakdown behind the totals. The file is generated in your
      browser, so the data does not leave Atlassian to produce it.
  - q: What is the difference between at risk and over budget?
    a: >-
      Two percentages you set per report. At risk defaults to 80 percent of budget consumed and over
      budget to 100 percent. They drive the health lozenge, the colour of the gauge, and the sort order
      on the home screen, so the reports that need attention rise to the top.
  - q: How far ahead does the forecast go?
    a: >-
      As far as you choose: to the report's end date, or 30, 60 or 90 days from today. You can also turn
      the forecast off. The projection is a linear run rate from the spend recorded so far, shown against
      the budget so any overrun or headroom is visible.
  - q: Can I see the work behind a number?
    a: >-
      Yes. Every report carries a parameters row spelling out the exact scope, method and currency it
      used, and clicking a bar or slice in a breakdown opens the matching work items in Jira.
  - q: Is there a free version?
    a: >-
      OnBudget is a paid app with a free trial, sold through the Atlassian Marketplace. See the listing
      for current pricing, which is where it stays accurate.
