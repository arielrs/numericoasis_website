Kindelve is a creature-collection dungeon descent, played in a browser. You are the Warden of a
rift in the ground. You send creatures down it, they fight their way deeper on their own, and your
job is to decide what goes down, what it carries, and when to stop.

It is a game about a single decision repeated: how deep is deep enough. One floor further is always
worth more, and always more likely to end the delve with nothing.

## The shape of a session

A delve runs on its own. You pick a Kin, pack what it carries, and send it down. From then on the
creature fights without instruction, and you spend what it earns while it is still falling deeper.

- The deep is made of **floors**, numbered from one, and the creature walks down through them.
- Every tenth floor holds a **Keeper**, which is the wall that ends most delves.
- Fighting earns **ichor**, which is spent inside that delve and gone when it ends.
- Ending a delve banks **echoes**, which are permanent and pay for everything that lasts.

A delve lasts minutes, not hours. What lasts is everything it leaves behind.

## What carries over

Nothing inside a delve survives it. Everything the delve finds does.

| What | Belongs to | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Echoes | The account | The permanent currency, spent on Warden skills |
| Relics | The account | Each copy stacks, up to a cap |
| Mutations | One Kin line | What makes your Crumb different from another player's |
| Creatures and eggs | The account | New lines to send down |
| Records and deeds | The account | The deepest floor reached, and what you did on the way |

## Where it runs

Kindelve runs in any modern browser, on a phone or a desktop, at
[play.kindelve.com](https://play.kindelve.com). There is nothing to install, though it can be added
to a phone's home screen.

The game is in open testing. It is free, it carries no advertising, and nothing in it can be bought
with money.

## The five languages

The game is playable in English, Portuguese (Brazil), Spanish, French and Japanese, chosen in
Options. Creature and place names stay in English in every language, deliberately: they are names
rather than words, and a Warden reading in Portuguese still needs to recognise what a Shieldback is.
