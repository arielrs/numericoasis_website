*Game industry layoffs in 2026 have already passed the total for 2025. For a small team, the useful question is not how bad it is, but how to plan a game that survives it.*

Game industry layoffs in 2026 reached **10,977 jobs by October 20**, according to the ASGC Games Industry Layoffs Tracker run by analyst Amir Satvat. That is more than the 9,197 lost in all of 2025, with about 35 studios closed and 161 hit by layoffs ([TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/114442/games-industry-layoffs-hit-10977-in-2026-already-surpassing-all-of-2025-with-months-to-go/index.html)).

The money has moved too. Gaming companies raised more than $1.6 billion in private financing in the third quarter of 2026, but AI and tools took more than 80% of it ([Drake Star](https://www.drakestar.com/news/global-gaming-report-q3-2026)). Studios making games are mostly funding themselves.

That does not make small games a bad idea. It means the plan has to work without a rescue: a scope the team can finish, more than one source of income, and costs that are known before the first line of code.

## How many game industry jobs were lost in 2026?

The ASGC tracker counted 10,977 job losses through October 20, 2026, and projects about 13,077 for the full year. That would still fall short of the 2024 record of 15,631. Other trackers use different methods and report lower totals, so compare figures only within one tracker.

| Year | Jobs lost (ASGC tracker) |
|---|---|
| 2024 | 15,631 |
| 2025 | 9,197 |
| 2026, to October 20 | 10,977 |
| 2026, projected | about 13,077 |

Developers feel it directly. In GDC's 2026 State of the Game Industry survey of more than 2,300 professionals, **28% had been laid off in the past two years** (33% in the US), and half said their current or most recent employer had made layoffs in the past 12 months ([GDC](https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more/)).

## Where does funding for small game studios come from in 2026?

Mostly from the founders. In the same GDC survey, **more than a third of game workers relied mainly on self-funding** for their projects, with publishing deals next, and co-development, private investment and venture capital limited ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/self-funding-rises-among-game-workers-as-layoffs-persist-and-union-interest-grows/)). These are the realistic options:

1. **Your own savings and service work.** Contract work for other studios is a common way to pay for your own game, and there is demand for it: in GDC's November 2026 report, 45% of studios had used co-development services in the past 12 months ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/layoffs-ai-and-next-gen-what-2-600-game-professionals-told-gdc-this-year)).
2. **Publishers, for fewer games.** "Most smaller publishers don't fund the production of games anymore," in the words of developer Johannes Grünewald, quoted by [SUPERJUMP](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/state-of-indie-2026-part-3-finance-and-tech/). Our reading is that a publisher is more likely to fund marketing, porting or the last stretch of a game that already exists.
3. **Public funds.** They are slow and competitive, but they do not take equity. In the EU, the Creative Europe call for video games and immersive content (CREA-MEDIA-2027-DEVVGIM) offers up to **€200,000 per project** from a €13.5 million budget, for European production companies that have commercially released a work since January 1, 2024. Applications close on February 2, 2027 ([Vandal](https://vandal.elespanol.com/noticia/2026293015/esta-ayuda-europea-ofrece-hasta-200000-euros-para-creadores-de-videojuegos-requisitos-y-como-solicitarla/)). In Brazil, when it signed the October 2026 decree regulating the games legal framework (Law 14.852/2024), the government announced a R$200 million public call for startups and innovation projects, a Rouanet Games program for sponsorship under the Rouanet Law, and a proposal to cut the minimum FSA audiovisual fund loan from R$500,000 to R$150,000 ([Meio & Mensagem](https://www.meioemensagem.com.br/midia/governo-federal-regulamenta-marco-legal-dos-games)).
4. **Venture capital, rarely.** Only 12% of gaming VC in the first half of 2026 went to game development, according to PitchBook figures cited by [SUPERJUMP](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/state-of-indie-2026-part-3-finance-and-tech/). Unless your company is building a tool, plan as if this door is closed.

## How should a small studio scope a game in 2026?

Scope the smallest game that proves its hook, can be finished with money the team already has, and can be shown to players early. In practice, that means a playable demo within months, not years, and a release plan that survives without a publisher, a grant or a feature from a store.

Some concrete rules we find useful:

- **Pick a genre the platform already buys.** Chris Zukowski's review of early-2026 Steam releases advises against the sword-and-adventure game as a first project, because it needs high production values and years of work ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/05/14/2026-q1-games/)).
- **Build a demo, not a free prologue.** The same review recommends a demo, and Steam Next Fest is built around demos.
- **Set a decision point.** Decide in advance what result from the demo means continue, change or stop. A wishlist or player target written down before launch is harder to argue with afterwards.
- **Reuse what you already know.** Your existing engine, codebase and pipeline are worth more than a fashionable new stack.
- **Design for the long tail.** More than half of PC and console playtime goes to games over six years old ([Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/?p=28834)), so plan updates and a game that rewards returning, not just a launch week.

## Which costs can a small game studio control?

People are almost all of it. A small studio's budget is mostly salaries or founders' time, which is why scope is the main lever. The rest is small but worth knowing before you start:

| Cost | What it is in 2026 |
|---|---|
| Steam Direct fee | $100 per game, recouped after $1,000 in adjusted gross revenue ([Steam Direct](https://partner.steamgames.com/steamdirect)) |
| Unity | Personal is free up to $200,000 in revenue and funding; Pro costs $2,310 per seat per year since a 5% rise on January 12, 2026 ([Unity](https://unity.com/products/pricing-updates)) |
| Store commission | Depends on the store and the route; mobile web purchases have their own fees by country |
| Tools and services | Hosting, source control, analytics, translation, legal and accounting: small monthly amounts that add up |

Our rule of thumb: count runway in months of the full team, not in money, and review it every month.

## A checklist for small studios in a contracting industry

1. Know your runway in months, and set the date by which the game must earn or the plan must change.
2. Keep a second income line, such as contract or co-development work, so the game is never the only bill payer.
3. Apply to one public fund whose deadline you can actually meet, with a project that already has a playable build.
4. Publish early and measure: a store page, a demo, real players.
5. Keep the team small until the game proves it needs more people.
