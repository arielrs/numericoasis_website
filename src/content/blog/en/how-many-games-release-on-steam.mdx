*How many games release on Steam used to be a trivia question. In 2026 it is the first fact a small studio has to plan around.*

How many games release on Steam? About **21,000 in 2025**, and **27,286 in 2026 by November 18**, according to SteamDB figures compiled by [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/11/steam-is-officially-drowning-in-game-releases/). The 2026 total passed all of 2025 in mid-October, when [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/steam-valve-slop-ai-genai-vibe-coding-pc-game-releases-2000746749) counted an average of 66 new games a day, up from 55 the year before.

The pace then jumped. ICO Partners counted **1,656 launches in a single recent week**, more than twice the previous weekly record of 757, and about 40% of those games had no user reviews when they were tallied ([Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/28193/weekly-steam-releases-surpass-1656-more-than-doubling-previous-record)).

Attention does not grow with the release count. Most of these games are seen by almost no one, so a launch plan has to start from that fact, not from the hope of being featured.

## How many games are released on Steam per year?

Steam had about 18,400 releases in 2024, about 21,200 in 2025, and more than 27,000 in 2026 by mid-November. Counts differ slightly from one source to another, but every source shows the same steep climb.

|---|---|---|
| 2024 | 18,424 | SteamDB, via [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/28193/weekly-steam-releases-surpass-1656-more-than-doubling-previous-record) |

## Why do most Steam games get so few players?

Because the number of players and their time grow far more slowly than the number of games. Of the 5,971 games released in the first quarter of 2026, **63.2% had fewer than 10 reviews** by early April, and only 1.6% had more than 1,000 ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/05/14/2026-q1-games/)).

Revenue is just as concentrated. A study by Totally Human Media and partners found that half of all games ever released on Steam earned less than $3,558, and that the top 1% of games took 84.6% of all earnings ([Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2026/09/01/the-median-revenue-of-games-on-steam-has-been-declining-for-12-consecutive-years)). The study was published on the site of a Steam search tool, so read it as a careful estimate rather than Valve data.

There is a less gloomy reading. Chris Zukowski of How To Market A Game estimates that roughly 75% of releases come from first-time developers, hobbyists or low-effort projects. A finished, well-presented game is not competing with all 27,000.

## What did Valve change for Steam developers in 2026?

Two changes matter most. In January, Valve narrowed its AI disclosure to content that ships to players, which we cover in [AI in game development in 2026](/blog/ai-in-game-development-2026/). In November, it tightened the pre-release review because of the flood of submissions.

The current [review process](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/review_process) asks you to:

- launch the build with the green Play button in Steam and run it for **at least five minutes**, within the past 7 days or since your last build;
- write a save file to Steam Cloud from the submitted build, if your store page lists Steam Cloud;
- tag **at least five screenshots as gameplay**;
- confirm the common redistributables your game needs and that your achievements are ready ([Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/151261/valve-steam-store-process-update)).

Valve asks for store pages to be submitted at least 7 business days before you want them live. Build that margin into the plan, more if submissions keep rising.

## How do you launch a game on Steam so it gets seen?

Start the store page early, collect wishlists, show a demo once in Steam Next Fest, and release only after passing review on the first try. The order matters more than any single step:

1. **Pay the Steam Direct fee.** It is $100 per game, recouped once the game earns at least $1,000 in adjusted gross revenue. For your first titles there is a 21-day wait between paying and releasing ([Steam Direct](https://partner.steamgames.com/steamdirect)).
2. **Publish a Coming Soon page as soon as you can talk publicly.** Valve requires at least two weeks of Coming Soon before release, and recommends going live earlier, once the art direction and core features are settled ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/coming_soon)).
3. **Collect wishlists.** Valve says wishlists "can be an important factor in determining where your game appears on Steam". Wishlisters get an email or notification at launch and when you run a discount of at least 20%. There is no minimum count before Steam shows a game, and Valve warns there is "no formula to accurately predict sales from wishlists" ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/wishlist)).
4. **Use Steam Next Fest once, with your strongest demo.** A game may appear in Next Fest only once. The next edition runs from February 22 to March 1, 2027, with registration closing on January 10 ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest/feb_2027)).
5. **Pass review on the first try.** Run the new checklist before you submit, not after a rejection.
6. **Keep talking after launch.** Valve notes that many players wait for an update or a discount before buying, so a wishlist is a long-term asset.

## What a small studio can control on Steam

Our view, after reading the data above: you cannot control the volume, but you control most of what decides whether a player stops on your page.

- **Fit the platform.** Zukowski's advice from the first-quarter data: learn Steam's own culture, and avoid scope-heavy genres such as the sword-wielding adventure for a first project.
- **Make a demo, not a free prologue.** That is also his recommendation, and it is what Next Fest is built around.
- **Show the game.** Screenshots that show play, a trailer that starts with play, and a short description that says what the player does.
- **Avoid giant launches.** Do not release in the same weeks as a release the whole market is talking about.
- **Measure before you commit.** Review wishlist data after every event and sale to see what actually moved people, as Valve suggests.

## The short version

Steam had about 21,000 releases in 2025 and more than 27,000 in 2026 by mid-November, so most new games get almost no attention: of the games released in early 2026, 63% still had fewer than 10 reviews in April. Valve's rules reward the same few things: a Coming Soon page published early, wishlists, one strong Next Fest demo and a build that passes the new pre-release checklist the first time. A small studio cannot change the volume, but it can choose its genre and scope, show real play, and time its launch away from the giants.
