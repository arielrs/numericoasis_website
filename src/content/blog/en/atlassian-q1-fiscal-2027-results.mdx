*An earnings release is not a product announcement, but it says where Atlassian is putting its effort. This one points firmly at Cloud and AI.*

Atlassian's Q1 fiscal 2027 results, [released on November 5, 2026](https://investors.atlassian.com/news/news-details/2026/Atlassian-Announces-First-Quarter-Fiscal-Year-2027-Results/default.aspx) for the quarter ended September 30, show revenue of $1,772 million, up 24% year over year, with Cloud revenue of $1,302 million, up 31%. Data Center revenue was $391 million, and Atlassian now expects it to fall by about 16% over the full fiscal year.

For admins, the useful signals are about direction rather than money. Atlassian's own figures show Cloud, Rovo and the bundled Collections growing, and Data Center customers buying less as they plan their moves. The shareholder letter is almost entirely about agents, the Atlassian MCP Server and the Teamwork Graph.

Investors focused on something else: the forecast that subscription growth will slow. [SiliconANGLE reported](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/05/atlassian-shares-fall-17-as-slower-subscription-growth-forecast-overshadows-earnings-beat/) that the shares fell about 17% in after-hours trading.

## What did Atlassian report for Q1 fiscal 2027?

For the quarter ended September 30, 2026, Atlassian reported total revenue of $1,772 million (up 24%), Cloud revenue of $1,302 million (up 31%), Data Center revenue of $391 million, and Marketplace and other revenue of $79 million. Subscription ARR reached $6,812 million, up 23%, and remaining performance obligations $4,746 million, up 44%.

| Measure | Q1 FY27 | Change year over year |
|---|---|---|
| Total revenue | $1,772M | up 24% |
| Cloud revenue | $1,302M | up 31% |
| Data Center revenue | $391M | up from $373M |
| Marketplace and other | $79M | up from $62M |
| Subscription ARR | $6,812M | up 23% |
| Remaining performance obligations | $4,746M | up 44% |

GAAP earnings were $0.35 per diluted share; non-GAAP, $1.65. Free cash flow was $463 million, and Atlassian returned $581 million through share repurchases. Figures are from the [results release filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001650372/000165037226000049/ex991q1fy27.htm).

## What is the outlook for Data Center?

Atlassian expects Data Center revenue to decline by about 14% year over year in Q2 and about 16% across fiscal 2027. SiliconANGLE reports that Atlassian tied tougher comparisons to Data Center purchases pulled into fiscal 2026 after the retirement announcement, and said Data Center customers are expanding less as they plan cloud moves.

The dates behind that are unchanged from the [September 2025 announcement](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/atlassian-ascend): March 30, 2028 is the last date for existing customers to buy new Data Center licenses, apps and expansions, and on March 28, 2029 Data Center and associated Marketplace app licenses expire and become read-only.

For the rest of fiscal 2027, Atlassian guides to total revenue growth of about 13.75%, Cloud growth of about 26% and Subscription ARR growth of about 18.5%. Q2 revenue is guided to $1,825 million to $1,835 million.

## What do the results say about Rovo and agents?

The [Q1 shareholder letter](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/company-news/shareholder-letter-q1fy27) reports that Rovo agent users doubled in six months, Rovo agentic automations grew ninefold year over year, and more than 80% of highly regulated Cloud customers have Rovo enabled. The rebuilt Atlassian MCP Server has more than 220 tools, over 2 million monthly users and more than 15 million daily tool calls.

The bundles are growing too. Service Collection, Atlassian's bundle for service teams, has passed 70,000 customers and $1 billion in ARR. More than 3,000 customers have upgraded to Teamwork Collection, seven times as many as a year earlier.

Other items worth noting:

- **Marketplace:** lifetime sales passed $7 billion, with more than 2,000 partners and more than 6,000 apps, integrations and agents.
- **Public sector:** Atlassian plans to submit for IL5 in July 2028, six months earlier than planned, with FedRAMP High in January 2029, and to submit Rovo for FedRAMP Moderate in Atlassian Government Cloud by June 2027.

## What does this mean for admins and buyers?

Our read, clearly labeled as opinion: nothing in the release changes your configuration, but it explains the direction of the products you run. Investment and messaging are concentrated on Cloud, agents and Collections. Expect renewals to come with Collection offers, and expect more Rovo and agent features in Jira, Confluence and Jira Service Management, each needing a governance decision from you.

Two cost changes already on the calendar make this concrete:

- **Cloud list prices** changed on October 13, 2026 for new purchases, renewals and upgrades, according to Atlassian's [pricing FAQ](https://www.atlassian.com/licensing/future-pricing/cloud/list/faqs). Quotes generated before that date are honored until they expire.
- **Usage-based billing** for Rovo credits and Automation steps starts on December 3, 2026, according to Atlassian's [usage billing documentation](https://support.atlassian.com/subscriptions-and-billing/docs/usage-charges-and-billing/). Atlassian's Rovo credits page says pay as you go is enabled by default, with an admin-defined cap available.

## What to do now

1. **Check your renewal date and quote** against the October 13 price change, and ask whether a Collection offer actually fits your mix of apps.
2. **Set usage limits before December 3.** Decide whether extra Automation and Rovo usage should be allowed, and who gets alerted. The mechanics are covered in [how Jira Automation usage limits work](/blog/jira-automation-usage-limits/).
3. **Decide your Rovo stance on purpose.** Rovo usage now has a billing consequence, so agree who may build agents and which content they can reach before adoption grows on its own.
4. **If you run Data Center, put the two dates in the plan**: March 30, 2028 for last purchases and March 28, 2029 for end of life.
5. **If you serve US public sector teams**, track the FedRAMP and IL5 dates above before committing to a Cloud timeline.

## The short version

Atlassian's Q1 fiscal 2027 results show Cloud growing 31% and Data Center forecast to shrink about 16% this fiscal year, with the company's attention on Rovo, agents and Collections. Nothing changes on your site because of the report, but your next renewal, the December 3 usage billing start and any Data Center plan should reflect that direction. If Data Center is still in your estate, [what a Jira Data Center to Cloud assessment should actually tell you](/blog/jira-data-center-to-cloud-assessment/) is a practical place to start sizing the move.
