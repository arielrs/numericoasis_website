*AI in game development is now ordinary in some jobs and a liability in others. The line between the two is mostly about what the player ends up seeing.*

AI in game development is widespread but divisive. In GDC's 2026 State of the Game Industry survey of more than 2,300 professionals, **36% said they use generative AI tools in their job**, 30% at game studios and 58% at publishers, support and marketing firms. Yet **52% said generative AI is having a negative impact** on the industry, up from 30% a year earlier and 18% the year before ([GDC](https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more/)).

On Steam, the share of new games that declare AI content keeps rising. A community analysis of about 63,600 games found disclosures on 13.2% of 2024 releases, 24% of 2025 releases and **40.1% of 2026 releases so far**, reaching 49.9% in October 2026 ([Power Up Gaming](https://powerupgaming.co.uk/2026/11/02/almost-half-of-steams-october-releases-disclosed-ai-use/)). These are self-reported disclosures counted by a Reddit user, not Valve figures.

The rules are clearer than the debate: Steam wants to know what ships to players, US copyright protects only human authorship, the EU now requires certain AI content to be marked, and performers' contracts require consent.

## Where do game studios actually use AI?

Mostly behind the scenes and in art: code assistance, testing, text and translation, and concept or placeholder images. Far less of it ends up as content generated live while a game runs. The pattern holds in the disclosures: of the Steam statements analyzed, 63% covered artwork, 30% text and translation, and only 3% content generated during play ([Power Up Gaming](https://powerupgaming.co.uk/2026/11/02/almost-half-of-steams-october-releases-disclosed-ai-use/)).

Large companies are aiming at production work. Square Enix set a goal of having generative AI handle 70% of its quality assurance and debugging by 2027 ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/final-fantasy-gen-ai-square-enix-qa-bugs-2000641688)). Disclosure is also far more common among small releases: in the same Steam analysis, 47% of 2026 games with fewer than 10 reviews disclosed AI use, against 14% of games with at least 1,000 reviews.

## What does Steam's AI disclosure require in 2026?

Steam's content survey asks about two kinds of AI content. **Pre-Generated** content is anything made with AI tools during development that "ships with your game and is consumed by players". **Live-Generated** content is created while the game runs, and for it you must also describe the guardrails that stop it producing illegal content ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey)).

Tools that only make development faster are out of scope. Valve's form says that "efficiency gains through the use of these tools is not the focus of this section." Valve rewrote the form in January 2026 to stop requiring disclosure of development tools such as coding assistants, while keeping it for generated content, including marketing and concept assets ([GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260120-steam-updates-ai-disclosure-guidelines)).

In practice: if AI made an image, a voice line, a piece of text or a translation that players will meet, disclose it. If AI only helped you write code or work faster, Valve is not asking.

## How do players react to AI in games?

Warily, and sometimes harshly. Among Steam games with at least 50 reviews, titles that disclosed AI averaged 77% positive reviews against 83% for those that did not, though that gap does not prove AI caused it ([Power Up Gaming](https://powerupgaming.co.uk/2026/11/02/almost-half-of-steams-october-releases-disclosed-ai-use/)). Players also cannot opt out: Steam does not let users filter AI-disclosed games out of new release lists, a gap [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/steam-valve-slop-ai-genai-vibe-coding-pc-game-releases-2000746749) has criticized.

Awards and festivals can be stricter than stores. In December 2025, the Indie Game Awards withdrew Game of the Year and Debut Game from Clair Obscur: Expedition 33, because AI-generated placeholder textures had shipped in the launch version, against rules that make games using generative AI ineligible ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/gaming/the-indie-game-awards-snatches-back-two-trophies-from-clair-obscur-over-its-use-of-generative-ai-164730842.html)). The assets had been patched out within days. It did not matter.

## Can you copyright AI-generated game assets?

Not in the United States, if they are purely AI-generated. The US Copyright Office's January 2025 report says prompts alone are not enough human authorship, while human-authored work that is perceptible in the output, and creative arrangements or modifications of AI output, can be protected ([US Copyright Office](https://newsroom.loc.gov/news/copyright-office-releases-part-2-of-artificial-intelligence-report/s/f3959c36-d616-498d-b8f9-67641fd18bab)).

The courts agree so far. On March 2, 2026, the Supreme Court declined to hear Thaler v. Perlmutter, leaving in place the ruling that human authorship is a "bedrock requirement of copyright" ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/the-final-word-supreme-court-refuses-to-hear-case-on-ai-authorship)). For a studio, an asset nobody can own may be an asset anyone can copy. Keep records of what people on the team designed, drew, wrote and changed.

## What do the EU AI Act and performer contracts require?

In the EU, Article 50 of the AI Act applies from **August 2, 2026**. Providers of generative AI must mark outputs in a machine-readable way, and deployers must disclose deepfakes, meaning images, audio or video that resemble real people, places or events and would falsely appear authentic ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content)). A voiced character built to sound like a real actor is exactly the kind of case to check with a lawyer.

Performers have their own rules. In the US, the 2025 SAG-AFTRA Interactive Media Agreement, ratified with 95% in favor, requires consent and disclosure for the use of digital replicas ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/video-games-strike-ends-after-11-months-as-sag-aftra-ratifies-new-contract/)), and sets pay of at least 7.5 times scale for performances used for real-time generation ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/sag-aftra-video-game-deal-details/)).

## A practical AI policy for a small game studio

This is our suggestion, and it fits on one page:

1. **List the tools and the jobs.** Write down which AI tools the team uses and for what: code, tests, translation drafts, concept art.
2. **Decide what may ship.** For each kind of asset that reaches players, decide whether AI output is allowed, and who reviews it.
3. **Track placeholders.** Tag every AI placeholder in the project and check the list before each release build.
4. **Disclose accurately.** If AI content ships, describe it plainly in Steam's survey and anywhere else that asks.
5. **Keep authorship records.** Keep sources, sketches and edit history that show human work.
6. **Get consent in writing.** No voice, face or performance is cloned without a contract that says so.
7. **Read each event's rules.** Festivals and awards may ban what stores allow.

## The short version

In 2026, about a third of game professionals use generative AI at work, about half think it is hurting the industry, and roughly 40% of new Steam games declare AI content. Steam asks you to disclose AI content that ships to players or is generated live, not the tools that only speed up development. US copyright still requires human authorship, the EU requires marking and deepfake disclosure from August 2026, and performer contracts require consent. Use AI where it saves time behind the scenes, decide deliberately what reaches players, disclose it honestly, and keep the records that prove the human work.
